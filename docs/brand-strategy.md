## Proposed strategic frame

### Product category and audience

**Category:** A campus-scoped marketplace for university students to buy and sell individual items.

**Primary audience (product scope, not researched persona):** University students using a selected campus to discover or list items. Buyers and sellers share the marketplace, while staff and couriers interact through narrower operational roles.

Do not invent demographic, lifestyle, income, device, or motivation claims about these people. Those details have not been researched.

### Positioning draft

> Soldbay is a campus marketplace for university students to find and trade individual items, with clear steps for listing, payment, and handoff.

This describes the product's approved scope and intended workflow. It does not claim the workflow is implemented, that transactions are risk-free, or that Soldbay is unique in the market.

### Proposed brand role

Help students understand what is available on their campus and what happens next when they buy or sell.

### Promise direction for discussion

**Working promise:** “Campus buying and selling, with the steps made clear.”

The promise is about intended communication and process clarity. It must not be rendered as a guarantee that every transaction succeeds, every seller is safe, support resolves cases within an unapproved time, or refunds arrive immediately.

### Differentiation boundary

The PSS defines a particular combination of campus-scoped discovery, buyer-specific negotiation, held funds subject to provider approval, manual courier coordination, code-confirmed handoff, and defined dispute handling. Whether that combination is distinctive or valuable to students is unvalidated. Do not use “only,” “first,” “safest,” “scam-proof,” or similar comparative/safety claims without evidence and review.

## Proposed brand principles

These are criteria to test during exploration, not approved personality attributes:

1. **Campus-aware:** Make the selected campus and availability context easy to recognize without implying student identity has been independently verified for every buyer.
2. **Plain about process:** Explain prices, next actions, responsibility, timers, pending states, and outcomes in direct language.
3. **Approachable in discovery, precise in transactions:** Browsing can feel welcoming; payment, verification, handoff, disputes, and refunds must read as exact and calm.
4. **Honest about limits:** Show when a step is pending, conditional, staff-operated, or dependent on an external provider. Avoid certainty beyond the PSS and external approvals.
5. **Useful across roles:** Keep the consumer identity coherent while allowing staff and courier tools to prioritize operational clarity and role-specific access.

## Voice and copy criteria

- Use familiar, direct English for v1; keep one name for each status and action across buyer, seller, and staff paths.
- State who acts next and what is waiting when an order is pending.
- Use precise money and timing language; distinguish item price, courier fee, held amount, refund pending, and refund confirmed.
- Use a calm, respectful tone for verification, disputes, failed handoffs, and account recovery. Do not blame users or promise outcomes before they occur.
- Avoid hype, fake urgency, unsupported safety claims, invented student slang, and marketing superlatives.
- Legal notices and final user-facing policy copy remain subject to privacy/legal review.

## Visual exploration criteria

Use these criteria to compare later territories on the same representative content and task:

| Criterion                             | What a candidate direction needs to support                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Campus and item clarity               | Users can recognize the active campus context and scan real listing photos, titles, prices, condition, and availability.                                                               |
| Transaction-state clarity             | Draft, negotiable, locked, unavailable, sold, pending, received, disputed, returned, and refund states remain distinguishable. Meaning must not rely on color alone.                   |
| Price confidence through legibility   | Public listing price, buyer-specific accepted price, expiry, and checkout total (including courier fee) cannot be confused. Do not imply a financial guarantee through visual styling. |
| Calm for consequential actions        | Verification, payment, code handoff, dispute, return, and refund screens should make required action and consequence clear without decorative urgency.                                 |
| Authenticity without fabricated proof | Use real, approved brand/product assets only. Do not invent student portraits, campus endorsements, testimonials, partner marks, badges, transaction counts, or success statistics.    |
| Cross-surface coherence               | The identity can carry from iOS/Android discovery to desktop and full-capability mobile staff workflows while preserving role clarity and density needs.                               |
| Accessible expression                 | Text remains readable; contrast and focus are considered; interactive targets suit platform guidance; status also has text/shape/icon cues. Exact conformance target remains open.     |
| Extendable system                     | Direction can support varied campus names, product photography, long titles, low-content states, busy operational queues, and future campuses without relying on one decorative motif. |
